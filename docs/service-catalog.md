# vCISO service catalog — 30 business problems

These are proposed engagement scopes. Delivery windows are agreed after kickoff, access and scope are confirmed. Measures below are acceptance candidates, not historical client results.

## 01. Keep the business running

Know which services matter most, where they can fail and whether recovery actually works.

**Target window:** 30–60 day scoped readiness sprint.

**Deliverables:** Critical-service and dependency map; Ransomware and crisis-response exercise; Restore test with observed recovery gaps.

**Candidate measure:** Measured restore time and data loss against agreed service objectives.

**Executive decision:** Which service must recover first, and who can authorize that recovery?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical operations stop | Prioritize essential services, dependency failures and continuity decisions. |
| 2 | Ransomware and cyber extortion | Review attack paths, containment choices, restoration and crisis ownership. |
| 3 | The business cannot recover | Exercise restoration and record missing dependencies, data loss and failed attempts. |
| 7 | Cyber or AI failure threatens physical safety | Work with process and safety owners on operating boundaries and escalation. |
| 28 | Security operations cannot respond effectively | Test detection, escalation, communications and response authority. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/sovereign-disconnected-cloud-security/blob/main/docs/contribution-19-restoration.md)

## 02. Protect customers, funds and IP

Connect identity, transaction authority and application controls to the information and money at risk.

**Target window:** 30–60 day priority-control review.

**Deliverables:** High-value data and action-authority map; Privileged and machine-identity review; Authorized application tests and remediation evidence.

**Candidate measure:** Priority access paths tested, excessive privileges removed and findings retested.

**Executive decision:** Who may see this record or authorize this transaction, and what blocks misuse?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Cyber fraud and executive impersonation | Review independent verification and high-risk transaction approvals. |
| 5 | Customer or regulated information is exposed | Trace sensitive data, access boundaries, disclosure and response obligations. |
| 6 | Trade secrets or proprietary AI are stolen | Define ownership and access controls for code, models and proprietary data. |
| 14 | Human and machine identities have excessive access | Review human, service and agent privileges, revocation and emergency access. |
| 26 | Unknown assets and legacy weaknesses remain unmanaged | Inventory the scoped estate and prioritize exposure with service owners. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/secure-coding-examples)

## 03. Deploy AI with accountable control

Make AI use cases, data boundaries and agent permissions reviewable before they become business authority.

**Target window:** 45–90 day bounded AI pilot.

**Deliverables:** AI inventory, risk tiers and accountable owners; Model, retrieval and tool threat model; Evaluation, approval, monitoring and rollback gates.

**Candidate measure:** Scoped use cases reviewed, allowed/denied cases exercised and unresolved risks assigned.

**Executive decision:** What can the AI access or do, and which decision must stay with a person?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | AI deployment creates regulatory exposure | Establish use-case applicability, accountable review and supporting evidence. |
| 10 | AI agents act beyond their authority | Restrict tool actions, identities and data reach; define approval and revocation. |
| 11 | Shadow AI and unknown agents | Identify AI use within the agreed estate and define an admission process. |
| 12 | AI applications can be manipulated | Exercise prompt, retrieval, output and tool boundaries in authorized tests. |
| 13 | Models or datasets are compromised | Review model/data provenance, component admission and change control. |
| 15 | AI produces unreliable business decisions | Set task-specific evaluation and human review for consequential outputs. |
| 16 | AI harms customers or employees | Define impact, fairness and safety evaluation with accountable domain owners. |
| 17 | AI decisions lack accountability | Assign owners, trace actions and establish review and challenge routes. |
| 18 | AI becomes unsafe or unreliable after deployment | Define monitoring, re-evaluation triggers, rollback and stop criteria. |
| 19 | AI use violates privacy or intellectual-property rights | Review data provenance, rights, confidentiality and legal applicability. |
| 20 | AI-generated software introduces weaknesses | Apply secure design, dependency review and meaningful regression checks. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/enterprise-ai-security-architectures)

## 04. Reduce dependency surprises

Make critical suppliers, shared dependencies and exit constraints visible to the people accepting the risk.

**Target window:** 30–60 day critical-supplier review.

**Deliverables:** Critical supplier and upstream dependency map; Evidence-based assurance and exception register; Continuity, concentration and exit decision brief.

**Candidate measure:** Critical dependencies with owners, reviewed evidence and tested or documented alternatives.

**Executive decision:** Which shared supplier failure can stop the service, and what is our fallback?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | A critical supplier fails | Identify continuity options and ownership for high-impact dependencies. |
| 21 | Vendor risk is poorly understood or monitored | Tie assurance requests, contracts and review cadence to supplier criticality. |
| 22 | Hidden suppliers create common points of failure | Map material upstream dependencies and concentration assumptions. |
| 23 | Supplier financial or geopolitical exposure threatens delivery | Escalate ownership, jurisdiction and continuity questions with specialist input. |
| 25 | Software and hardware supply chains cannot be trusted | Review provenance, approved components, signed admission and lifecycle support. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/sovereign-disconnected-cloud-security/blob/main/docs/dependency-inventory.md)

## 05. Retain control of critical technology

Define sovereignty across data, operators, keys, suppliers and recovery—not just the location of a workload.

**Target window:** 30–90 day architecture and validation scope.

**Deliverables:** Sovereignty and landing-zone boundary register; Disconnected operating and transfer contracts; Key-custody, exit and post-quantum dependency plan.

**Candidate measure:** Critical external dependencies identified and agreed local-operation boundaries exercised.

**Executive decision:** Who can operate, decrypt, update, support and recover the service?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | The business lacks control over data and technology | Review jurisdiction, operator authority, key custody, portability and recovery. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/sovereign-disconnected-cloud-security)

## 06. Make risk and investment explainable

Turn fragmented security activity into owned decisions, an investment case and evidence leadership can challenge.

**Target window:** 10–30 day decision baseline.

**Deliverables:** Board risk brief and accountable risk register; Requirements-to-control-to-evidence map; Costed options and a prioritized 90-day roadmap.

**Candidate measure:** Priority risks with owners, explicit assumptions, funded decisions and dated evidence.

**Executive decision:** Which business loss are we treating, why this investment and what will show progress?

| Original # | Business problem | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | Leadership cannot see total enterprise risk | Connect service impact, dependencies, risk appetite and treatment decisions. |
| 29 | Security spending lacks demonstrable value | Compare treatment options, costs and loss assumptions; test control effectiveness. |
| 30 | AI investment fails to produce financial returns | Define business-value hypotheses and stage gates before further investment. |

[Related public reference work](https://github.com/cybersmeashish/ashish-vciso-repo)
